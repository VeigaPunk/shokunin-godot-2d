## Three lessons (cross-note synthesis of the 19 reviewed notes)

1. **Identity is not appearance.** Several notes make the same point from different angles: a key's physical position is not its printed label (keyboard note), a teleported body needs `reset_physics_interpolation()` because presentation state is not simulation state (teleport note), and a plausible generated image is not authored intent (notes 64/86/68/81). Practical rule: in Godot 2D code, name which identity a value carries (physical vs. logical, simulated vs. rendered) before comparing or saving it.
2. **Boundary correctness says nothing about the interior.** Note 98 (a seamless boundary is not a right interior; a faster solver gives a different answer), note 42 (SDF/MSDF corners vs. thin features), and note 55 (handles vs. constrained vertices) all warn that passing the easy edge check can hide interior error. Practical rule: every fixture needs at least one interior probe, not only seams, corners, or endpoints.
3. **Control and fidelity are separate dials.** Note 90 (particle control vs. physical fidelity) and the dither-array notes (94/102/107/111/115/120, periodic arrays with graded provenance) show that art-directable behaviour and physically or historically faithful behaviour are different targets. Practical rule: state which dial a technique is tuned for before judging it "correct."
