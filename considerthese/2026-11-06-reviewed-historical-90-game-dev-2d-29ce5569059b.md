# 2D: particle populations and controllable motion

[Reeves, 1983](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs448-01-spring/papers/reeves.pdf) represents fuzzy objects through evolving particle populations. Birth, attributes, movement and death are procedurally controlled, with stochastic variation constrained by parameters.

[Sims, 1990](https://karlsims.com/papers/ParticlesSiggraph90.pdf) develops composable particle operations. Applied velocity can alter position without changing the stored velocity; its vortex has adjustable influence and explicitly favors animator control over physical simulation.

The foundation remains useful for population-level organization. The later approach adds a vocabulary for combining motion treatments without demanding that every artistic effect emerge from forces alone. This is a control-versus-physical-fidelity tradeoff, not a claim that arbitrary compositions are independent or realistic.

Both are historical graphics methods, not verified features or performance of today's 2D engines. Selected retained introductions and motion passages only; formulas and implementations not audited.
